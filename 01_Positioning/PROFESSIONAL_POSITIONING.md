# 1. Purpose

This document defines the single, consistent positioning narrative that must appear — in tone, structure and core claims — across every external-facing asset: Executive CV, LinkedIn profile, Executive Biography, interviews, professional networking, and any future consulting profile.

Earlier positioning drafts (01_Positioning/) were written **before** the Executive Case Repository and the two Phase 3 methodology documents existed. They correctly identified *what* to say (manufacturing executive, Industry 4.0 evolution) but could not yet say *why it's credible* — because at the time there was no documented evidence of a repeatable methodology behind it. This document closes that gap.

---

# 2. The Core Differentiator

Most manufacturing executive candidates are positioned by **titles held** and **years of experience**. This is the weakest possible differentiator at the executive level, because it is shared by every competing candidate with a similar CV.

Alfredo's positioning is different in kind, not just in degree:

> He is not defined by the plants he has managed. He is defined by a documented, repeatable engineering decision methodology (see ENGINEERING_DECISION_FRAMEWORK.md) that has produced a permanent operational standard in every one of seven independent projects, across four companies, three industries and two decades — combined with a leadership operating system (see EXECUTIVE_OPERATING_SYSTEM.md) that makes that methodology implementable through people, not just on paper.

This is the sentence every other document in the Career Portfolio must be traceable back to.

# 3. Executive Positioning Statement

Alfredo Merejildo is a Manufacturing and Operations Executive with nearly two decades leading industrial operations across multinational and national companies in plastics, food, mining equipment and industrial manufacturing.

He does not solve isolated operational problems. He applies a consistent engineering methodology — observe without assumptions, model the system, identify the true constraint, quantify before deciding, and standardize the result — to convert operational challenges into permanent competitive advantages, regardless of the specific technical domain.

More recently, this same methodology has been applied to a new domain: Industrial Digital Transformation, through the design and development of BNQTA, an Industrial Functional Intelligence Platform integrating Artificial Intelligence, Industrial IoT and predictive analytics.

# 6. Positioning the Industry 4.0 / BNQTA Period

This is the single highest-risk section of the entire Career Portfolio, because it is the part most likely to be misread by a recruiter as a career change or an employment gap. The positioning must be unambiguous every time it appears:

**What it is NOT:** a pivot into software development, a career change, or a break from manufacturing leadership.

**What it IS:** the same engineering methodology, documented across seven manufacturing cases, deliberately applied to a new class of problem — industrial systems that report *what happened* but not *why*, or *what is likely to happen next*.

**Supporting evidence to use consistently:**
- The idea originated from a real operational limitation observed directly during plant leadership (a commercial OEE system that only counted output, and never explained equipment behavior).
- BNQTA achieved external, third-party validation: Top 10 (Startup UNI Pre-Incubation, ~30 teams) and Top 4 (Startup UNI Incubation, ~35 teams) — this is objective evidence, not self-assessment, and should be used in the CV and LinkedIn exactly as it appears in the source record.
- Alfredo's own stated view of AI reframes the narrative usefully: AI is valuable only when combined with domain judgment, field experience and the ability to ask the right question to isolate the real problem — the same posture as an engineer, not a technologist chasing a trend. This is a differentiating point of view, not just a technical skill, and should appear in interviews and the Executive Biography as a distinguishing perspective on Industry 4.0 — most operational candidates either avoid AI entirely or oversell it; Alfredo's stated position is neither.

# 9. Reframing Technical Independence for External Audiences

Flagged previously as a risk: the raw statement "I am not someone who simply follows orders when I have a different, evidence-based view" is honest and true to the Executive Operating System, but read cold in a CV or first LinkedIn impression, it risks sounding difficult to manage rather than valuable.

**Positioning rule going forward:**

- **Internally (interviews, once technical credibility is established, executive biography in full):** the direct version is appropriate and differentiating — it signals seniority and integrity.
- **Externally, first impression (CV headline, LinkedIn summary, cover letter opening):** reframe the same trait around its outcome rather than its friction —

> "Known for challenging assumptions with evidence rather than opinion, and for proposing technically superior alternatives that still meet the original business objective — not for resisting direction."

This is not softening the claim; it is leading with the same fact's business outcome (see ENGINEERING_DECISION_FRAMEWORK.md, Section 4 — Decision-Reconciliation Pattern) instead of its friction, which is where a first-impression document should always start.
