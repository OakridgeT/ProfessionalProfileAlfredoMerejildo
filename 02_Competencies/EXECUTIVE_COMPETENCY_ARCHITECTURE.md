# 1. Purpose

This document defines the executive competency architecture that supports Alfredo Merejildo's professional positioning.

Competencies are organized into strategic capability domains reflecting the requirements of executive manufacturing leadership. As of v2.0, every domain is anchored to at least one documented Executive Case, so that ATS keywords and CV claims can be traced back to verifiable evidence rather than asserted in general terms.

This framework is the primary source for ATS optimization, Executive CV generation, interview preparation and career positioning — and works in conjunction with ENGINEERING_DECISION_FRAMEWORK.md (methodology) and PROFESSIONAL_POSITIONING.md (narrative).

# 7. Operational Risk & Safety Engineering

## Purpose

Design safer, more reliable and sustainable industrial operations through systematic risk identification, engineering controls and organizational prevention strategies.

### Competencies

- IPERC (Hazard Identification, Risk Assessment and Control)
- Process Risk Assessment
- Incident Investigation
- Root Cause Analysis
- Ergonomic Improvement
- Emergency Planning
- Environmental Risk Reduction
- Engineering Controls
- Safe Work Systems
- Industrial Hygiene Fundamentals
- Operational Reliability
- Regulatory Compliance
- Safety Culture Development

### Case Evidence

| Case | Evidence |
|---|---|
| CASE-005 | Eliminated rotating-tool hazards, airborne dust, and heat exposure by replacing mechanical grinding with a 300-bar industrial water cleaning system — engineering elimination of hazard rather than administrative mitigation. |
| CASE-002 | Removed ergonomic overload and dust exposure from manual Calcium Carbonate handling by automating the process. |
| CASE-006 | Redesigned workstations (material carts, suspended sewing equipment, waist-height handling) to remove physical strength as a job barrier, improving ergonomics for the entire workforce. |

### Executive Perspective

Safety is not treated as an independent compliance function. It is integrated into operational design, engineering decisions and continuous improvement initiatives — consistent with the Engineering Decision Framework principle that hazard elimination is preferred over hazard mitigation whenever the process itself can be redesigned.
