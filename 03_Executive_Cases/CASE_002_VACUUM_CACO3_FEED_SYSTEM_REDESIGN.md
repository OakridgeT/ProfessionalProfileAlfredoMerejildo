# Executive Summary

Led the redesign of the Calcium Carbonate (CaCO₃) feeding system for the PVC compound preparation process by repurposing an existing vacuum conveying infrastructure that had remained unused for years.

Instead of mitigating the operational consequences of manual material handling, the project eliminated the root cause by automating the feeding process without requiring major capital investment.

The solution permanently improved ergonomics, housekeeping, operational reliability and process consistency, while becoming the new operating standard for the plant.

---

# 1. Operational Context

PVC compound preparation was the first critical stage of the manufacturing process.

Every production batch supplied the downstream extrusion and injection operations, meaning that any interruption or instability at this stage immediately affected manufacturing continuity.

The Lima operation consisted of:

- PVC extrusion plant
- PVC injection plant

The compound preparation area supplied both manufacturing processes.

Monthly production was approximately:

- **2,200 metric tons** for extrusion.
- **210 metric tons** for injection.

The injection plant manufactured the complete portfolio of PVC fittings for Peru, supplying approximately **30% of the national market**.

Because the mixing plant was the starting point of the entire production chain, improvements at this stage produced organization-wide operational benefits.

---

# 2. Initial Situation

Calcium Carbonate (CaCO₃) represented the largest-volume raw material used in the formulation.

Material loading was performed manually.

Operators transported bags from storage, opened them manually and poured the material into the mixer.

This operating method generated several problems simultaneously:

- excessive airborne dust;
- poor housekeeping conditions;
- ergonomic overload;
- operator dependency;
- unnecessary handling time;
- process variability.

Previous improvement initiatives had focused on reducing dust through extraction systems rather than eliminating its source.

---

# 3. Business Objective

Improve the material feeding process while increasing operational efficiency and reducing occupational exposure.

The objective was not only to improve housekeeping but to redesign the operation in a way that simultaneously increased process robustness, operator safety and production reliability.

---

# 4. Root Cause Analysis

Rather than accepting the existing operating method as unavoidable, the complete material handling system was analyzed.

During the engineering review an unused vacuum conveying pipeline was identified.

Further investigation confirmed that:

- the conveying line remained physically installed;
- the pipeline had been intentionally isolated years earlier;
- the vacuum filter had sufficient capacity;
- the PLC still supported the required operating sequence;
- the existing infrastructure could potentially transport Calcium Carbonate without major equipment replacement.

The analysis demonstrated that the plant already possessed the fundamental infrastructure required for automated feeding.

The limitation was not technological but conceptual.

---

# 5. Executive Decision

Instead of investing in new equipment or mitigating dust through additional extraction systems, the decision was made to recover and repurpose the existing vacuum conveying system.

The strategy focused on eliminating the source of the operational problem rather than reducing its consequences.

This engineering approach minimized investment while maximizing operational impact.

---

# 6. Stakeholder Management

Successful implementation required coordination between multiple organizational functions.

Internal stakeholders included:

- Production
- Maintenance
- Manufacturing Engineering
- Operators
- Logistics

External support included equipment suppliers and technical consultation when required.

Operational personnel participated throughout implementation to ensure that the redesigned process could be integrated into daily production without disrupting manufacturing continuity.

---

# 7. Implementation

The project required redesigning both the material handling sequence and the operating philosophy.

Major activities included:

- evaluation of the existing vacuum conveying infrastructure;
- restoration of the isolated conveying line;
- verification of filtration capacity;
- PLC sequence review;
- process validation;
- operating parameter optimization;
- industrial production trials;
- operator training;
- controlled implementation.

The solution was developed almost entirely by leveraging existing plant assets rather than purchasing new equipment.

# 9. Executive Impact

The project transformed a manual, operator-dependent activity into an automated and standardized industrial process.

The solution became the permanent operating standard for the Lima plant.

Rather than representing an isolated productivity improvement, the project demonstrated how existing industrial assets could be reinterpreted to create new operational capabilities without major capital expenditure.

---

# 10. Legacy & Knowledge Transfer

The redesigned feeding system remained as the official operating standard after implementation.

Although the solution was not replicated elsewhere within the Aliaxis Group—primarily because other plants did not necessarily possess the same vacuum conveying infrastructure—it established an important internal precedent:

existing assets should be evaluated as potential engineering opportunities before new investments are considered.

This project reinforced a decision-making philosophy centered on engineering creativity, operational understanding and capital efficiency.

---

# 11. Executive Thinking

The project was based on a simple engineering question:

*"Is the current solution really necessary?"*

Instead of treating manual feeding as an unavoidable process constraint, the entire system was reconsidered from first principles.

The objective was not to improve manual loading.

The objective was to eliminate manual loading.

By challenging an accepted operating assumption, an existing but forgotten capability of the plant was transformed into a permanent competitive advantage.

The project demonstrates a recurring engineering philosophy:

**Eliminate the cause instead of mitigating the symptom.**
